Using reflective models critically

Reflective models can provide useful structures and starting points, but they are guides rather than rigid procedures and may not suit every situation [1].

Advantages

  • Provides a structure to work through.
  • Offers a starting point when you are unsure how to begin.
  • Encourages consideration of different aspects of a situation.
  • Gives the reflection a recognisable sense of completion.

Limitations

  • Can imply that stages must always be followed in a fixed order.
  • Real situations may not begin neatly at the model's first stage.
  • A particular model may not fit every experience.
  • Reflection is ongoing rather than truly ending when the model ends.

Different models work for different people and circumstances. Treat them as tools rather than rules; as your reflective practice develops, the most useful approach may change. If a model is not helping, another approach may be more appropriate [1].
